## Description
```
Saturn's main rings span about 273,560 km — 70% of the way from Earth to the Moon — yet in most places they're only about 10 meters thick.
Up close they're countless chunks of almost pure water ice, and all of it together would make a ball smaller than the moon Mimas.

Visualization based on real data:
• Ring edges and gaps: PDS Ring-Moon Systems Node (French et al. 2017 radius scale); outer edge of the A ring 136,770–136,780 km
• Pan (~28 km wide) keeps the Encke Gap open (NASA)
• Typical thickness ~10 m (NASA); optical depth and particle albedo by ring: PDS, NSSDC
• Paper analogy: 10 m / 273,560 km = 3.7×10⁻⁸ — a 0.1 mm sheet at that ratio is a disc ~2.7 km across
• Particle sizes: dust to ~house-sized (NASA); power law q ≈ 3, sharp cutoff near 4–5 m (Marouf et al. 1983; Brilliantov et al. 2015)
• Orbital speed in the A ring 16.7–17.6 km/s (≈60,000 km/h) from Saturn's GM (JPL); neighbors drift at ~mm/s (Ohtsuki 2025)
• Ring mass (1.54 ± 0.49)×10¹⁹ kg = 0.41 Mimas masses (Iess et al. 2019) → a ball of solid ice (917 kg/m³) ~318 km wide (280–348 km within the mass error); Mimas is 396 km
• "Ring rain": an Olympic pool's worth every half hour; the rings could be gone in ~300 million years (NASA 2018)
```
