## Description

The star is gone. Its expanding blast is still reshaping the gas around it.
SN 1987A's bright knots reveal a collision with material expelled long before the star exploded.

Visualization based on real observations: the blue-supergiant progenitor,
the 1987 event, the early neutrino detections, and the shock-heated
circumstellar ring.

Sources:
- Blue-supergiant progenitor and shock interaction: https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/shock-wave-sheds-new-light-on-fading-supernova/
- 1987 event and neutrinos preceding the first visible-light observation: https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/webb-finds-evidence-for-neutron-star-at-heart-of-young-supernova-remnant/
- Ring older than the explosion, bright shock-heated knots, inner ejecta: https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/webb-reveals-new-structures-within-iconic-supernova/
- Observed evolution of the knots: https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/supersonic-shock-wave-heats-gas-ring-around-supernova-1987a-sn1987a/
- Asymmetric ejecta: https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso1032/
- Infrared colour assignment and observed morphology: https://esawebb.org/images/SN1987a-1/

Rendered in Blender Cycles. Artist's reconstruction: procedural gas,
interpreted colours, unmeasured small-scale structure, and compressed
time. Stellar close-ups and remnant views use different spatial scales;
camera paths do not represent a physical journey between epochs.
The blue stellar texture is an illustration, not a resolved image of the
progenitor. Telescope images and documentary footage are not included.
Music is original procedural synthesis; narration is Kokoro af_heart.
