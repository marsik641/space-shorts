## Description

```text
In 2022, NASA deliberately crashed DART into Dimorphos — and changed its orbit.
The escaping debris pushed back, strengthening the spacecraft's shove.
This was a planetary defense test; neither asteroid threatened Earth.

Visualization based on real data:
• Impact: September 26, 2022, at approximately 6.1 kilometers per second.
• Dimorphos orbits the larger asteroid Didymos.
• Nominal volume-equivalent diameters: Dimorphos 151 m; Didymos 761 m. Published pre-impact dimensions of Dimorphos: approximately 177 × 174 × 116 m.
• Mean orbital distance between the bodies' centers: approximately 1,189 m before and 1,152 m after.
• Over the following weeks, its orbital period settled at 11 hours, 22 minutes and 3 seconds — 33 minutes and 15 seconds shorter than before, according to the 2024 analysis.
• Recoil from escaping rock and dust amplified the change in momentum.
• DART was humanity's first planetary defense test.

Sources:
• Impact date, orbital distances and period change — NASA/JPL, March 19, 2024:
https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-study-asteroids-orbit-shape-changed-after-dart-impact/
• Impact speed, ejecta recoil and first planetary defense test — ESA, October 12, 2022:
https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Hera/First_kinetic_impact_test_succeeds_in_shifting_asteroid_orbit
• Observed rock-and-dust ejecta and volume-equivalent sizes — Li et al. 2023, Nature 616, 452–456:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-05811-4
• Dimorphos dimensions from DART imaging — Dotto et al. 2024, Nature 627, 505–509, Methods / Dimorphos shape constraints:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06998-2
• Neither asteroid posed a threat to Earth — NASA, December 15, 2022:
https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/early-results-from-nasas-dart-mission/

Rendered in Blender Cycles · scientific visualization / artist's impression.
Stone materials include AI-generated texture work; this is reconstructed imagery, not mission footage.
Bodies and orbital distances share a common physical scale. Mesh shapes, surface detail, orientations and viewpoints are reconstructed, not exact photogrammetry. Approach and impact are slowed; debris evolution and weeks of orbital change are compressed. Orbital motion is accelerated to one circuit in about eight screen seconds. The orbit diagram uses circles for clarity; the real post-impact orbit is slightly eccentric. Debris paths illustrate ballistic ejection and recoil, not an N-body solution. A consistent Sun direction is used, with soft fill approximating sunlight reflected from Dimorphos; illumination and exposure are not photometrically calibrated. Rock and dust are sunlit, without a fireball. Music and impact sound are artistic sound design. The final approach repeats the pre-impact moment to create a visual loop; it is not a second collision.
```
