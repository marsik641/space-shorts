## Description
```
That's not a moon — it's TRAPPIST-1c, seen from its neighbor b at closest approach: almost 2.5× wider than our full Moon.
Seven Earth-sized planets, all inside a space smaller than Mercury's orbit, 40 light-years away.

Visualization based on real data:
• Star TRAPPIST-1: 0.119 R☉ (≈1.16 Jupiter radii), 2566 K, 40.7 light-years (Gaia parallax 80.2 mas)
• Planets b–h: 0.76–1.13 Earth radii, years of 1.51–18.77 days, outermost orbit 0.062 AU (Mercury: 0.387 AU)
• From b at closest approach, c spans 1.27° (full Moon ≈ 0.52°); from e the star spans 2.17° (our Sun: 0.53°)
• Period ratios ≈ 8:5, 5:3, 3:2, 3:2, 4:3, 3:2 (resonant chain)

Sources:
• Star and planet parameters, periods, radii, orbits — Agol et al. 2021, Planet. Sci. J. 2, 1 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01074
• Distance — Gaia EDR3 via SIMBAD — https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=TRAPPIST-1
• Discovery of seven planets — Gillon et al. 2017, Nature 542, 456 — https://doi.org/10.1038/nature21360 ; NASA/JPL — https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-telescope-reveals-largest-batch-of-earth-size-habitable-zone-planets-around-single-star/
• Resonant chain — Luger et al. 2017, Nature Astronomy 1, 0129 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04166
• Habitable zone (e, f, g) — NASA — https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/trappist1/
• b and c: no thick atmospheres (JWST) — Greene et al. 2023, Nature 618, 39 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14849 ; Zieba et al. 2023, Nature 620, 746 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10150 ; Gillon et al. 2026, Nature Astronomy — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02128
• e: hydrogen atmosphere ruled out, verdict open — Espinoza et al. 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05414 ; Glidden et al. 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05407 ; Allen et al. 2026, AJ 171 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07695
• Jupiter, Mercury, Moon — NASA NSSDC fact sheets — https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/

system maps the star is enlarged ×3 and planets are markers, time runs 1 day = 2 s (×43,200), periods are real,

background stars brightened.
```
